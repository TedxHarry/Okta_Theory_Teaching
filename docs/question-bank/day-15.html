Day 15: Questions

Connected responsibilities and incident context

Q01. Connect responsibilities

Question: A company sources employee profiles from Workday into Okta. Its AD directory supplies designated work email and validates passwords on a configured delegated path. Applications separately manage accounts, sign-in checks, permissions, and sessions. How would you explain these responsibilities as a connected journey?

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

Start with the employee information governed by Workday and its correct association with the Okta identity. Okta connects that identity to approved groups and assignments. AD supplies the designated email and checks passwords on the stated delegated path.

Applications then evaluate their sign-in information, account state, permissions, and sessions. Naming each job explains why one successful system result cannot establish every later outcome. Follow the person's requirement through those boundaries instead of merely listing product names.

Q02. Contractors in an HR-led environment

Question: A company uses Workday-led employee sourcing. Priya’s sponsor-approved contractor information is maintained in Okta; checks show no Workday association or AD assignment, and employee imports exclude her population. How can this contractor model coexist with the employee architecture?

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

Source priority applies to actual source associations; it does not connect every person to every source. Priya follows the stated Okta-managed process without an invented HR relationship.

Her population label alone would not enforce that separation. Here the checked links and import scope support it. Explain the maintenance mechanism and its responsibility so an HR-led environment is not mistaken for a requirement that every identity must follow the employee path.

Q03. Different protocols, different outcomes

Question: In one company, Salesforce uses SAML for sign-in, Expense uses OpenID Connect authorization code with a backend exchange and ID token validation, and Projects uses SCIM for account management. What different outcomes do these connections support, and what do they not prove for each other?

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

SAML supplies sign-in information Salesforce must validate. OpenID Connect supplies Expense's identity exchange, which its backend must validate before associating the intended account. SCIM supports Projects account-management requests.

These purposes differ. Salesforce's sign-in protocol does not establish its provisioning behavior, and Expense entry cannot verify a Projects create. Follow each operation to its receiver, then check account, permission, and session outcomes relevant to that application's requirement.

Q04. Current and archived context

Question: Jordan has departed in current access-removal tickets. An archived password incident explicitly concerns a separate time when he was employed. Devi is now Finance, although older examples describe Sales. How should an investigator use these current and historical facts?

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

Keep the attempt or effective point beside each conclusion. Current tickets use current requirements; the archive supplies the state for its historical investigation.

Diagnosing an old password incident does not authorize restoring Jordan's present access. Likewise, an earlier independent example cannot replace a current observation. The same person can appear in different states without those records becoming one shared incident or supplying each other's missing results.

Trace a representation defect

Q05. Locate the wrong outgoing representation

Question: Devi’s approved Finance department must reach her existing Projects application account as FIN, while access continues. Workday HR and correctly sourced Okta data show Finance. The outgoing departmentCode mapping is fixed at SAL, preview and app profile show SAL, and a correlated successful update stores SAL on linked account prj-1042. Projects uses this value for allocation reporting. Where is the first demonstrated wrong representation, and did the target reject the transmitted value?

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

Start with the approved meaning: Devi is in Finance, so Projects needs FIN. Workday and Okta already hold the correct department. The fixed-SAL outgoing mapping is the first supplied step that turns it into the wrong representation.

The preview and app profile show that result, and the correlated update and later read show SAL was sent and stored. Projects accepted the request; it did not reject the value.

The operation can therefore succeed while the business outcome is wrong. Correct the conversion rather than change the correct source data or invent a failed target update. Verification must follow the corrected value through to the intended account and reporting result.

Q10. Make the evidence request specific

Question: Devi’s Projects allocation report needs FIN, but the preview, app profile, transmitted update, and later target read all show SAL. That names which records answer which questions. A useful request explains the result it would distinguish instead of asking for an unspecified pile of events. Keep the intended account and operation correlated throughout the comparison.

Separate access paths and approvals

Q11. Normal rule and independent assignment

Question: Priya is correctly classified as Sales/Contractor. Salesforce’s normal rule requires Sales AND Employee, processes her profile, and excludes her from its group. Salesforce nevertheless remains individually assigned; its exception approval, owner, scope, and end condition are not supplied. Is a defect in the normal employee-only rule demonstrated, and which separate path needs investigation?

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

The rule's supplied result is correct: a Contractor does not meet Sales AND Employee. The application assignment comes through a separate individual relationship.

Trace that relationship and request its business justification. Broadening the normal rule would change the requirement rather than explain the existing assignment. Nonmembership in one group does not deny every other path, so inspect assignment sources before deciding where a defect or approval gap exists.

Q12. Working access and missing approval

Question: Priya is correctly classified as Sales/Contractor. Salesforce’s normal rule requires Sales AND Employee, processes her profile, and excludes her from its group. Salesforce nevertheless remains individually assigned; its exception approval, owner, scope, and end condition are not supplied. Her active Salesforce account returns a protected page after accepted sign-in. Is working access proved authorized or proved unauthorized by the missing approval record?

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

Working access is established: Priya entered Salesforce and received a protected page. Its authorization is still unresolved because the packet supplies no exception decision, owner, scope or end condition.

Missing evidence is not the same as an authorized owner confirming that no approval exists. It also cannot be treated as approval. Ask that owner for the current decision and its terms.

Keep both findings in the handoff: access works, and the approval question needs prompt review. The next action depends on that decision rather than on a guessed rule defect or an invented exception.

Q13. Clarify the business wording

Question: A Salesforce request says “Keep useful contractor access; executives should be different.” Normal access is employee-only, and exceptions need approval. Contractor eligibility, actions, end conditions, and executive treatment are unspecified. Which decisions should the business owner clarify before automation?

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

Ask which contractors qualify, which actions they may perform, who approves and maintains their exception, and when it ends. Ask what different executive treatment actually means.

It could describe exclusion or a specific capability; it does not automatically authorize an authentication bypass. Record these questions with their owners. Technical configuration can implement an agreed decision, but it cannot supply the missing business meaning.

Q14. Keep application approvals separate

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

It supports the Expense approval and checked entry only. It cannot authorize Salesforce access or supply a Projects account-management result.

Preserve the success within its scope while requesting each application's missing evidence. The same contractor can have different approved paths and unresolved outcomes across services. Name the application and purpose so an approval or login for one destination does not become a person-wide access claim.

Q15. Conditional next paths for B

Question: Priya is correctly classified as Sales/Contractor. Salesforce’s normal rule requires Sales AND Employee, processes her profile, and excludes her from its group. Salesforce nevertheless remains individually assigned; its exception approval, owner, scope, and end condition are not supplied. Actual Salesforce entry works. If the decision stays unresolved, name the missing evidence and accountable owner. These are conditional paths, not supplied approval or correction results. Do not choose a conclusion simply to close the ticket when its business decision remains open.

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

Coordinate the Projects and Expense owners on their separate unresolved outcomes while identity management tracks the departure scope. Their investigations can proceed alongside other ticket work; priority does not require every owner to wait in one queue.

There is no need to invent an attack, financial deadline or detailed outage cause. State the observed retained access and the approved end requirement, then explain which results the owners must verify.

Record findings, proposals, and outcomes

Q37. Five statements in a useful update

Question: An investigator must hand off an identity ticket involving a business requirement, several observed system results, and a proposed correction that has not been performed. What five statements would make the update understandable to someone who did not watch the investigation?

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

Give the next reader five answers: what should have happened, what was observed, what explains any demonstrated discrepancy, what was done or handed off, and what result was verified.

For this question, the correction is only proposed. Say so, identify the responsible owner, and name the evidence still needed. Do not turn 'we know the next step' into 'the problem is fixed.'

Include the person, application instance and relevant attempt or effective point. That makes the update understandable without the investigation history and keeps unresolved work visible to whoever must finish it.

Q38. Unresolved can be the right result

Question: One identity ticket has working contractor access but no supplied approval decision. Another has a create conflict with unknown resource ownership. Why can unresolved be the right conclusion, and how can each open result still guide useful work?

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

The first needs an accountable approval decision; the second needs ownership and operation-history evidence. Neither gap is solved by choosing a familiar technical fix.

Name the missing evidence and the person who can decide or verify it. Uncertainty does not mean nothing was learned. A precise open question establishes the next step while preserving the findings already supported, without inventing approval, cause, or a completed correction.

Q39. A recommendation is not a retest

Question: A handoff recommends repairing a department mapping, resolving an absent Salesforce approval, retrying failed Projects removal, and terminating an Expense session. No corrective action or retest is supplied. May the report describe these recommendations as verified resolutions?

Read the teaching explanation

Explanation:

No. Recommendations identify proposed work and needed verification. They do not establish that an action occurred or met its requirement.

Existing successes at other boundaries remain useful, but cannot become later fixes for these issues. Use verbs carefully: propose, request, confirm, and verify describe different evidence states. A defensible handoff names the responsible owner and acceptance result still needed before reporting resolution.

Keep the original observation and add the new evidence beside it. Then reconsider the conclusions that depended on the earlier unknown ownership or history.

Explain what the application owner's verified account history now supports and what it still leaves open. A changed conclusion is appropriate when the evidence changes; preserving a diagnosis after its basis disappears would make the record misleading.

Update the next action, responsible owner and verification still required. The record should show why the explanation changed, without rewriting an earlier uncertainty as though it had always been known.
