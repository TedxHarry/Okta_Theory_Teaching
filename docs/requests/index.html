Working through requests

A request usually arrives as a symptom: “the app is missing,” “my phone changed,” or “the report is wrong.” Your first job is to turn that symptom into a question you can investigate.

Read each situation and choose your next checks before opening the reasoning. Explain why each check matters. If the evidence leaves something unknown, name the missing information instead of filling the gap with an assumption.

  • Reading records and settings: connect a question to the record or configuration that can answer it. Return to the relevant parts as you work through the lessons.
  • The application is missing: follow a user, group, and assignment without creating an unnecessary exception. Read after Day 5.
  • The department changed, but the report did not: distinguish account data from application permissions and decide what would close the request. Read after Day 14.
  • A replacement phone and a locked account: choose the appropriate recovery action and verification. Read after Day 14.
  • A connection needs new credentials: distinguish SAML signing trust from provisioning authorization. Read after Day 14.

The records and follow-up results are fictional. These situations are independent of the Northbridge timeline. A named setting or successful result in one situation does not establish that setting or result in another.

For each request, write the expected outcome, the supplied facts, the next useful check, the proposed action, and the evidence needed afterward. The reasoning sections explain acceptable conclusions; your wording does not need to match them.

Work through one stage at a time. Record your next check before expanding its reasoning or reading the next observation. When a follow-up supplies a result, update that case using the new evidence. When a changed situation replaces a fact, start a separate explanation rather than retroactively changing the original closure.
