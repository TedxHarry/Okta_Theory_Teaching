# Okta Theory

A person can sign in and still be unable to use an application. A department can be correct in one system and wrong in another. An employee can leave while an application continues accepting an existing session. Understanding these situations begins with knowing which system is responsible for each part of the journey.

This 15-day theory course follows Northbridge Services, a fictional company, through those questions. Alongside Devi, Daniel, Priya, Jordan and Alex, you will trace identity data, application assignments, authentication, account management and changing access. Each situation asks you to explain what the evidence establishes and what needs checking next.

This is a reading-and-reasoning course. It builds understanding and evidence-based troubleshooting habits. It does not require an Okta tenant, and it does not claim to replace hands-on configuration experience.

An **Okta tenant**, also called an **organization** or **org**, is a separate Okta environment with its own users and configuration. The product explanations use **Okta Identity Engine (OIE)**. When consulting other material, check which engine it describes before applying its policy terminology or behavior here.

## How to use it

For Days 1–14, use three parts in order:

- **Lesson**: the day's concepts, worked through one realistic situation at a time.
- **Exercises**: reasoning questions to attempt on your own.
- **Self-check**: model answers to compare against after you try the exercises.

Work the core days in order. Follow the lesson's situation first, then attempt its exercises before opening the answers. If your conclusion differs, compare the evidence and the missing distinction rather than memorizing the model wording. When you return to revise, use the [Revision Guide](revision/index.md). Each core day has a separate recap with short concept explanations, a familiar example, and questions to check your understanding.

“Day” identifies a lesson's place in the sequence; work at your own pace. No prior Okta administration or programming is assumed. Read the supplied message examples as evidence; you do not need to run them.

Use [your notebook](notebook/guide.md) to explain each situation in your own words. After Days 5 and 10, attempt the corresponding [checkpoint](assessments/index.md). Day 15 brings the concepts together in an independent final case: read its evidence, attempt the investigation tasks, then compare your reasoning with the answers and skills checklist. If an answer reveals a gap, revisit the relevant explanation and try again.

Treat each labelled evidence packet on its own terms. A successful example does not resolve an earlier ticket, and a hypothetical change does not rewrite the main story. Keep proposed actions separate from verified outcomes in your notes.

After the final case, the optional [Day 16 practice bridge](lessons/day-16-safe-practice-plan.md) helps you plan a small training experiment. It sits outside the 15-day theory core. You can complete its predictions without a tenant; actual execution requires an authorized training environment. It does not add a required assessment or replace hands-on configuration practice.
